# The Imperial Audit: British Colonial Rule in India (1757–1947)

An open-access digital archive and long-form investigative repository documenting one hundred and ninety years of British East India Company and British Crown governance across the Indian subcontinent.

---

## Executive Summary

The Imperial Audit is an evidence-first historical archive engineered to provide an unredacted chronicle of British imperial governance in South Asia from the Battle of Plassey (1757) to the Partition and Transfer of Power (1947).

Constructed from the parliamentary records of Hansard, the India Office Records at the British Library, the National Archives in Kew, and peer-reviewed macroeconomic research, this publication explores the structural mechanisms of colonial rule. Rather than presenting a generalized narrative, the archive examines specific administrative policies, fiscal drain apparatuses, engineered famines, legal declarations, and regional resistance movements through rigorous primary-source documentation.

## The Five Historical Dispatches

The archive is divided into five standalone long-form investigative monographs:

### Part I: The Corporate Takeover (1757–1857)
* **Route**: `/en/chapters/timer`
* **Subject**: The transformation of the English East India Company from a chartered joint-stock merchant enterprise into a territorial sovereign governing over two hundred million individuals.
* **Key Incidents**:
  * The Battle of Plassey (1757) and the Treaty of Allahabad (1765)
  * The Great Bengal Famine of 1770 and fiscal extraction policies
  * The Sannyasi and Fakir Guerrilla Rebellions (c. 1763–1800)
  * The Chuar and Tribal Uprisings in the Jungle Mahals (1767–1800s)
  * The Polygar Wars and the Tiruchirappalli Proclamation (1799–1801)
  * The Vellore Sepoy Mutiny (1806)
  * The Paika Rebellion in Odisha (1817)
  * The Kittur Uprising under Rani Chennamma (1824)
  * The First and Second Anglo-Sikh Wars and the Annexation of Punjab (1845–1849)
  * The Unilateral Annexation of Awadh (1856)
  * The Santhal Hul (1855–1856)
  * Macroeconomic Analysis of the Council Bills mechanism and the $45 Trillion Wealth Drain

### Part II: The Great Rupture (1857–1858)
* **Route**: `/en/chapters/comparison`
* **Subject**: A forensic dissection of the 1857 Uprising across northern and central India, exploring both its immediate military triggers and systemic structural causes.
* **Key Incidents**:
  * Enfield Pattern 1853 cartridge controversy and the General Service Enlistment Act
  * Mangal Pandey at Barrackpore (29 March 1857)
  * The Meerut Outbreak and the 40-mile march to Delhi (10 May 1857)
  * The Siege and Recapture of Delhi, the Kashmir Gate breach, and executions at Khooni Darwaza
  * The Sieges of Kanpur, the Satichaura Ghat incident, and the Bibighar reprisals
  * The Defense of Lucknow under Begum Hazrat Mahal
  * Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi, the escape from Jhansi Fort, and the battle at Kotah-ki-Serai
  * Veer Kunwar Singh and the guerrilla campaign across Bihar
  * The systemic scale of British civilian reprisals and mass executions
  * The Government of India Act 1858 and the abolition of the East India Company

### Part III: The Actors of Empire & Resistance
* **Route**: `/en/chapters/dossiers`
* **Subject**: Twelve comprehensive biographical monographs examining the key administrators of the imperial apparatus alongside the leaders of anti-colonial resistance.
* **Monographs Included**:
  * Robert Clive (1st Baron Clive of Plassey)
  * Warren Hastings (First Governor-General of Bengal)
  * Charles Cornwallis (Architect of the 1793 Permanent Settlement)
  * James Broun-Ramsay, 1st Marquess of Dalhousie (Author of the Doctrine of Lapse)
  * George Nathaniel Curzon (Viceroy of India and architect of the 1905 Partition of Bengal)
  * Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer (Amritsar Garrison Commander)
  * Sepoy Mangal Pandey (34th Bengal Native Infantry)
  * Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi
  * Begum Hazrat Mahal (Regent of Awadh)
  * Dadabhai Naoroji (Member of Parliament for Central Finsbury and author of the Drain Theory)
  * Bal Gangadhar Tilak (Editor of Kesari and Mahratta)
  * Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (Leader of the Non-Cooperation and Salt Satyagraha movements)

### Part IV: Ten Minutes in Amritsar (13 April 1919)
* **Route**: `/en/chapters/share`
* **Subject**: The definitive, minute-by-minute forensic investigation of the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre.
* **Key Sections**:
  * Wartime recruitment in Punjab and the passage of the Rowlatt Act (1919)
  * The arrests of Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew and Dr. Satyapal (10 April 1919)
  * The implementation of the Crawling Order and martial law
  * The gathering on Baisakhi (13 April 1919)
  * The firing: 1,650 rounds of .303 ammunition discharged in ten minutes
  * The Hunter Commission testimony and Dyer's admissions
  * Rabindranath Tagore's formal letter renouncing his knighthood
  * The House of Commons and House of Lords parliamentary debates
  * The 1940 assassination of Sir Michael O'Dwyer by Shaheed Udham Singh at Caxton Hall

### Part V: The Subcontinental Corpus & Regional Atlas
* **Route**: `/en/chapters/action`
* **Subject**: An interactive, multi-regional historical atlas organizing 190 years of events across twelve subcontinental territories, accompanied by a verified primary-source bibliography.
* **Regions Documented**:
  * Bengal Presidency (West Bengal and Bangladesh)
  * Punjab Province
  * United Provinces (Awadh and Agra)
  * Tamil Nadu (Madras Presidency)
  * Kerala (Malabar and Travancore)
  * Andhra (Northern Circars and Rayalaseema)
  * Assam and the Northeast Frontier
  * Gujarat and Bombay Presidency
  * Maharashtra and the Deccan Plateau
  * North-West Frontier Province (Peshawar and the Tribal Agencies)
  * Odisha and the Chota Nagpur Plateau
  * The Indian National Army (INA) and Armed Defense Movements

## Primary Archival Sources

All data points, casualty figures, dates, and administrative quotations are drawn directly from primary and academic references:

1. **India Office Records (IOR)**, British Library, London (Series L/PJ, L/PS, L/MIL).
2. **The National Archives (TNA)**, Kew, United Kingdom (Foreign Office and Cabinet Papers).
3. **Hansard Parliamentary Debates**, House of Commons and House of Lords (1757–1947).
4. **Report of the Committee Appointed by the Government of India to Investigate the Disturbances in the Punjab** (Hunter Committee Report), Command Paper Cmd. 681, His Majesty's Stationery Office, London, 1920.
5. **Report of the Commissioners Appointed by the Punjab Sub-Committee of the Indian National Congress**, 2 vols., Bombay, 1920.
6. **Dadabhai Naoroji**, *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*, Swan Sonnenschein & Co., London, 1901.
7. **Ramesh Chunder Dutt**, *The Economic History of India under Early British Rule* and *The Economic History of India in the Victorian Age*, Kegan Paul, Trench, Trübner & Co., London, 1902–1904.
8. **Utsa Patnaik**, *Revisiting the Drain, or Transfer from India to Britain in the Context of Global Diffusion of Capitalism*, Columbia University Press, New York, 2018.
9. **Amar Farooqui**, *Opium Inc.: How a Global Drug Trade Funded the British Empire*, Three Essays Collective, 2018.
10. **Sabyasachi Bhattacharya**, *The Financial Foundations of the British Raj*, Orient Blackswan, Hyderabad, 2005.

## Design System and Technical Specifications

### Visual Language
* **Background Canvas**: Deep Charcoal Obsidian (`#0A0A0A` / `var(--background)`)
* **Primary Typography**: Editorial Light Cream (`#F2F2F2` / `var(--text)`)
* **Accent Color**: Pure Signal Vermilion (`#FF0000` / `var(--accent)`)
* **Typography Stack**:
  * Serif: *Source Serif 4* (Google Fonts / Variable Font Weight)
  * Monospace: *JetBrains Mono* (Google Fonts / Tabular Figures)
* **Image Treatment**: Unframed historical newspaper aesthetic with CSS hardware-accelerated monochrome filtering (`filter: grayscale(100%) contrast(1.12) brightness(0.93)`).

### Technology Architecture
* **Framework**: Next.js 14 (App Router architecture with React Server Components)
* **Language**: TypeScript 5.6 (Strict Mode enabled)
* **Styling**: Tailwind CSS 3.4 with custom utility tokens
* **Icons**: Lucide React
* **Runtime**: Node.js 18+ / 20+

## Getting Started

### Prerequisites
* Node.js version 18.17.0 or higher
* npm version 9.0.0 or higher

### Installation

1. Clone the repository or navigate to the project directory:
   ```bash
   cd the-imperial-audit
   ```

2. Install the necessary dependencies:
   ```bash
   npm install
   ```

3. Initialize the development server:
   ```bash
   npm run dev
   ```

4. Open [http://localhost:3000](http://localhost:3000) in your web browser to view the application.
