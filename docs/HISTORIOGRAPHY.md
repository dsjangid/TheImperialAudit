# Historiographical Framework and Archival Methodology

This document outlines the methodological approach, archival sources, and historiographical debates underpinning the content of The Imperial Audit.

---

## 1. Methodological Mandate

The Imperial Audit operates under an evidence-based historiographical mandate. Rather than relying on secondary generalizations, every narrative incident, administrative decision, and casualty figure is grounded in:

1. Official government dispatches from the India Office and the Governor-General in Council.
2. Official parliamentary inquiries recorded in the Westminster Hansard.
3. Court transcripts, commissions of inquiry (such as the 1920 Hunter Commission), and contemporary legal papers.
4. Peer-reviewed macroeconomic data quantifying land revenue, commodity exports, and bullion flows.

---

## 2. Archival Collections Cited

### Primary British Government Repositories
* **The British Library, London (India Office Records)**:
  * Series `IOR/L/PJ`: Public and Judicial Department Records (1857–1947).
  * Series `IOR/L/MIL`: Military Department Records (Bengal, Madras, and Bombay Armies).
  * Series `IOR/L/PS`: Political and Secret Department Records (Princely States and Frontier Relations).
* **The National Archives (TNA), Kew**:
  * `CAB/23` and `CAB/24`: British War Cabinet Minutes regarding wartime Indian food supply and military mobilization (1914–1945).
  * `FO 371`: Foreign Office correspondence regarding revolutionary movements and trials.

### Primary Indian Nationalist and Commission Records
* **National Archives of India (NAI), New Delhi**: Home Political Files, Sedition Trial Records, and Confiscated Literature (1907–1947).
* **Hunter Commission Report (1920)**: *Report of the Committee Appointed by the Government of India to Investigate the Disturbances in the Punjab*, Cmd. 681.
* **Congress Punjab Inquiry (1920)**: *Report of the Commissioners Appointed by the Punjab Sub-Committee of the Indian National Congress*, 2 volumes, Bombay.

## 3. Key Historiographical Debates

### The Nature of Plassey: Battle vs. Financial Coup
* **Traditional British View**: A brilliant tactical triumph by Robert Clive against overwhelming numerical odds.
* **Modern Critical Historiography**: A pre-negotiated transfer of power orchestrated through bribery involving Mir Jafar, the Jagat Seth banking house, and East India Company agents.

### The Great Rebellion of 1857
* **Colonial Administration ("The Sepoy Mutiny")**: Depicted as an isolated, retrograde military mutiny prompted by religious fanaticism.
* **Nationalist Historiography ("First War of Independence")**: Characterized as a unified, conscious national struggle for liberation.
* **Subaltern and Contemporary Academic View**: A composite coalition of diverse agrarian, military, and aristocratic rebellions driven by overlapping socio-economic grievances rather than a singular centralized leadership.

### The Drain of Wealth Hypothesis
* **Dadabhai Naoroji (1901)**: First formulated the concept that India's persistent export surplus was intercepted as unrequited tribute without economic return.
* **Utsa Patnaik (2018)**: Applied econometric modeling to historical trade and balance-of-payments data, estimating that Britain extracted approximately 44.6 trillion dollars (in 2016 USD) between 1765 and 1938 through the Council Bills and Home Charges mechanisms.
